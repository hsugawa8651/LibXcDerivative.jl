How closely it matches Libxc

The kernels are translations, so the question that matters is how closely they reproduce the library they were translated from. The answer is recorded in the shipped files rather than only claimed here.

Two builds, because one question needs each

buildused for
the one Libxc.jl vendors, 7.0.0the corpus-wide comparison the test suite runs, which anyone can reproduce with ] add Libxc
a build of the pinned sources, plus a MAXORDER=4 onethe kernel-by-kernel comparison, and the third and fourth derivatives, which the vendored build does not implement

The vendored build is what the suite can rely on, and the one a reader can check for themselves. But it is a release, and the kernels here were translated from a development state after 7.1.2, so where upstream changed a functional in between, the vendored build is the one out of date. Eleven functionals are in that position, with the worst relative difference between the two libraries beside each:

These eleven are compared against the pinned sources instead (CORRECTED_AFTER_7_0_0 in test/corrected_after_7_0_0.jl). Six further identifiers are mixes holding one of them and inherit the difference.

What puts a functional in that table is the measurement and not the ChangeLog, because from inside the suite the two readings of a disagreement, upstream changing the functional or this package translating it wrongly, are the same observation: there is one library to call. Called against both, each of the eleven has the two libraries differing while this package follows the pinned one to 7.5e-15 or better. Three of the eleven have no ChangeLog entry naming them.

What the manifest records

src/generated/manifest.toml says once, at the top, which Libxc the corpus came from, and then repeats it per entry so that a partial regeneration cannot leave the file self-inconsistent: 441 copies, all checked against the one at the top by the suite.

julia> using TOML

julia> m = TOML.parsefile(joinpath(pkgdir(LibXcDerivative), "src", "generated", "manifest.toml"));

julia> m["upstream_version"], m["upstream_commit"][1:7]
("7.1.2", "5d5076f")

julia> using Dates

julia> DateTime(m["generated_at"]) isa DateTime
true

Each kernel entry carries an agreement table: the inputs used, the Libxc version measured against, when, and three figures per entry-point name and per slot. Every one of the 283 kernels has one, covering 543 entry-point names at orders 0 through 2: zk, vrho and v2rho2, which is as far as the pinned MAXORDER=2 build goes.

The three figures are the worst absolute difference over the sweep, that difference divided by the largest value the slot takes on it, and the worst of the per-point ratios. Two relative figures because neither alone is honest: the scaled one hides a point where the slot is small, and the pointwise one reports a zero crossing as a disagreement. Both are explained below, with what separating them is worth in practice.

Beside it, 275 of those kernels carry a high_agreement table of the same shape, covering 530 names at third and fourth order: the v3* and v4* slots, up to 55 of them for a meta-GGA. Those orders are measured against a MAXORDER=4 build of the pinned sources, since the library Libxc.jl vendors stops at second order. Four kernels with an energy have no high_agreement table, and the reason is upstream's: gga_x_wpbeh, mgga_x_r2scan, mgga_c_r2scan and mgga_x_r4scan are capped at second order in Libxc's own generator, their piecewise junctions being only C2 there, so there is nothing above second order to compare against.

The worst recorded differences, over all of it:

Above second order, where the measure has to be chosen first

The orders above second need a build configured for them, which the library Libxc.jl vendors is not; the manifest's high_agreement table holds them, measured against a MAXORDER=4 build of the pinned sources. Two things about those comparisons belong here, because each has already caught a wrong claim.

One figure scaled by the quantity, one taken pointwise, and both recorded. gga_c_pbe's v2rho2 changes sign at rho ≈ 0.7348. Two values that differ by 2.8e-17 there differ by 1.3e-10 relatively, so a pointwise relative difference reports the zero crossing rather than the agreement. Dividing the largest absolute difference over a sweep by the largest value on it gives 3.2e-15 for the same pair, which is where every other component of that tensor sits.

That scaled figure has the opposite failure, and it is larger than it looks. Where a slot is small at one point and large at another, dividing by the largest value hides how badly the small point did: gga_x_beefvdw's v3rhosigma2 reports 5.9e-08 scaled against 4.2e-06 pointwise, a factor of 71, and its v4rho2sigma2 a factor of 334. A BigFloat evaluation of that expression says it is ill conditioned rather than wrong, but 5.9e-08 is not what a caller differentiating it there gets. So both figures are written down, for every slot, and the pointwise one is never the smaller of the two.

At third order the sigma direction loses digits, and nothing else does. Over rho log-spaced on [1e-3, 10] with sigma = rho^(8/3), 20,000 points, scaled as above, against a MAXORDER=3 build of the pinned sources:

slotgga_x_pbegga_c_pbe
v3rho34.5e-154.6e-14
v3rho2sigma4.8e-153.7e-15
v3rhosigma27.3e-156.1e-15
v3sigma38.7e-131.1e-12

Differentiating three times in the one variable that enters only through s^2 is where the chain keeps subtracting nearly equal terms. It is the direction and not the functional: both of these show it, and the other three components of the same tensor stay where the second derivatives are.

Corpus-wide, 1e-12 is not the worst case, and the worst case is not this package's: over all three families, 530 names have a third or fourth derivative to compare, 98 of them are above 1e-12, and the worst is 4.4e-6 (mgga_x_br89_explicit, v4rhotau3). Evaluating the same expression in BigFloat shows neither side at fault there: that kernel is too ill conditioned for a fourth derivative in double precision. The bounds held at these orders are therefore per kernel rather than corpus-wide, 1e-12 for the gga_x_pbe and mgga_x_scan kernels and 1e-10 across the variants that come back from the same call.

Tolerance tiers

Everything is held to atol=1e-10 against Libxc, except for eight kernels and variant entry points that are allowed a relative bound of 1e-7 instead, with the measured worst case recorded beside each in LibXcDerivative.LIBXC_LOOSE_AGREEMENT.

Three things about that tier are deliberate:

  • The strict bound is the default. With the looser one as the default, a functional that began disagreeing by 1e-8 would ship with nothing noticing.
  • One figure for the whole tier, not one fitted per entry. Everything that misses 1e-10 lies between 2.7e-10 and 2.4e-8, so 1e-7 is where the boundary falls on its own. A figure chosen to admit a particular functional is a figure that admits whatever comes next, and gga_x_hjs_b97x, whose 1.1e-6 has no identified cause, is outside it and is not shipped at all.
  • A mix inherits its components' bound, read off the <name>_components the generated source carries rather than from a second list. The four mixes holding mgga_c_kcis miss atol=1e-10 by about 2e-9, which is that kernel's own 2.4e-8 arriving with its coefficient.

The table lives in the package rather than in the test suite, so everything that holds a functional to the rule reads the same copy of it.

Checking it yourself

The test suite compares against Libxc.jl's vendored library, so the comparison needs nothing but Julia:

julia --project=test test/runtests.jl

Every function named on this page is listed, with its full docstring, under Public methods.
